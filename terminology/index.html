Reference · Terminology

The words behind the history.

84 terms from the African diaspora, Ethiopianism and Pan-Africanism, and Rastafari, with their meanings. Where a meaning or origin is disputed, the entry says so.

Religious terms describe what communities believe, not historical fact, and meanings can differ between Rastafari houses and speakers.

ABCDEGHIJKLMNOPRSTUWZ

84 shown

A

AbyssiniaEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

The older, outside name for Ethiopia, used in English for centuries. The name appears in Black Atlantic religion and song.

African diasporaAfrican diaspora

The communities of African origin living outside Africa. The largest dispersal was the forced migration of the transatlantic slave trade, joined by later voluntary migrations.

African redemptionEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

The hope that Africa and its peoples will be restored to dignity and freedom, a theme of Garveyism, Ethiopianism and Rastafari.

AksumEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

An ancient kingdom of the Ethiopian highlands, a trading power that adopted Christianity in the fourth century under King Ezana.

AnansiAfrican diaspora

The spider trickster of Akan folklore. Anansi stories crossed the Atlantic and remain a core part of Jamaican storytelling.

ApprenticeshipAfrican diaspora

The system from 1834 to 1838 that kept most formerly enslaved people in Jamaica bound to unpaid or low-paid labour for their former owners.

B

BabylonRastafari

The biblical city of exile. For Rastafari it names the oppressive system of colonialism, racism and materialism, and the institutions that uphold it.

Back-to-Africa movementEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

Efforts across the nineteenth and twentieth centuries to return people of African descent to Africa, of which Garvey's programme was the most influential.

Battle of AdwaEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

The 1896 victory of Ethiopian forces over an invading Italian army, a rare decisive win by an African state over a colonial power, celebrated across the diaspora.

BinghiRastafari

A short form of Nyabinghi, used for the gatherings and the drumming.

Black AtlanticAfrican diaspora

A term popularised by the scholar Paul Gilroy for the shared, ever-moving culture linking Africa, the Americas and Europe across the Atlantic.

Black Star LineEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

A shipping company launched by Garvey's UNIA in 1919 to link the African diaspora. Financial troubles led to Garvey's conviction for mail fraud in 1923, a verdict many see as politically driven.

Bobo AshantiRastafari

A house of Rastafari, also written Bobo Shanti, founded by Prince Emanuel Charles Edwards and centred at Bull Bay near Kingston. Its members are known for turbans and robes.

Mansions of Rastafari

Brethren and sistrenRastafari

Rastafari terms for fellow members, men and women.

BurruAfrican diaspora

A Jamaican drumming tradition linked with festivals and the Kingston poor. Scholars cite it as an African-derived influence on Nyabinghi drumming.

The Sacred Drum

C

ChaliceRastafari

The pipe used by some Rastafari for the sacramental sharing of herb, a ritual object and not an everyday item.

Coral Gardens incidentRastafari

Violence near Montego Bay in April 1963 followed by a roundup of Rastafari across Jamaica. In 2017 the prime minister apologised in Parliament.

1963 on the timeline

CoromanteeAfrican diaspora

A colonial label for captives from the Gold Coast, mostly Akan speakers. It comes from the trading fort of Kormantin, so it names a port as well as a people.

CreolizationAfrican diaspora

The making of new cultures in the colonies from African, European and other sources. Scholars prefer it to "mixture" because the results are new things in their own right.

D

DownpressionRastafari

A Rastafari word replacing "oppression", for pressing people down.

DreadRastafari

A term for a Rastafari, especially one with locks, expressing awe or fear of God.

Dreadlocks (locks)Rastafari

Hair left to form locks, worn by many Rastafari as a sign of covenant and natural living, often citing Leviticus and the Nazirite vow. Not all Rastafari wear them.

Contested Scholars debate the sources of the style, including African traditions, Indian ascetics and the Mau Mau.

DuppyAfrican diaspora

In Jamaican folk belief, the spirit of a dead person.

Contested The word's African or other origin is uncertain.

E

EldersRastafari

Senior and respected members who guide worship and teaching. Rastafari have no central hierarchy, so authority rests on age, knowledge and standing.

EmancipationAfrican diaspora

The ending of slavery. In the British Empire it began on 1 August 1834, with full freedom in Jamaica in 1838. It is commemorated each year on 1 August.

EmpressRastafari

A title of respect for a Rastafari woman, echoing the Ethiopian empress and affirming women's dignity.

EthiopiaEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

An East African state with one of the world's oldest Christian traditions, never long colonised. In the diaspora it came to stand for African independence.

EthiopianismEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

A tradition of reading Ethiopia as the symbol of African dignity and of God's promise to Africans, first in Black churches and later in politics. It prepared the ground for Rastafari.

The Rastafari Awakening

Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo ChurchEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

The main Christian church of Ethiopia. "Tewahedo" means "made one", for its teaching on the union of the divine and human in Christ. Bob Marley was baptised in it in 1980.

Contested The church does not accept Rastafari belief in Haile Selassie's divinity.

Ethiopian World FederationEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

An organisation founded in New York in 1937 to support Ethiopia against Italy and to unite Black people worldwide, with local branches in Jamaica.

ExodusEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

The biblical story of Israel's escape from slavery, long used in the African diaspora as a picture of liberation and return.

G

Ganja (herb)Rastafari

Cannabis, used as a sacrament by some Rastafari in prayer and reasoning. Many Rastafari do not use it, and ritual use is distinct from recreational use. Jamaica's 2015 law allows sacramental use by Rastafari.

GarveyismEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

The ideas of Marcus Garvey: racial pride, economic self-reliance and African redemption. His movement had millions of followers.

1914 on the timeline

Ge'ezEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

The ancient language and script of Ethiopia and Eritrea, still the liturgical language of the Ethiopian Orthodox Church.

Grounation (Groundation)Rastafari

A Rastafari gathering of worship, reasoning and drumming. "Grounation Day" marks the 21 April 1966 visit of Haile Selassie I to Jamaica.

1966 on the timeline

H

Haile Selassie IEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

Emperor of Ethiopia from 1930 to 1974. His throne name means "Power of the Trinity". He was crowned on 2 November 1930, deposed in 1974 and died in 1975. Rastafari revere him as divine.

Contested His divinity is a Rastafari belief, not a historical finding.

1930 on the timeline

I

I and IRastafari

A Rastafari phrase for "I", "we" or "you", expressing the oneness of people with each other and with Jah.

Indentured labourAfrican diaspora

Work under a contract that binds a person to an employer for a set term. After emancipation, planters recruited indentured workers, including Africans, Indians and Chinese.

IrieRastafari

A Jamaican and Rastafari word meaning good, fine, all is well.

Contested Its exact origin is uncertain.

ItalRastafari

Natural, unprocessed food in the Rastafari way of life, from "vital". It is usually plant-based and cooked without much salt, though practice varies by household and by house.

Italo-Ethiopian WarEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

Italy's invasion of Ethiopia in 1935 and occupation from 1936 to 1941. It stirred solidarity across the Black world, including in Jamaica.

1935 on the timeline

IyaricRastafari

The Rastafari way of speaking, which reshapes English words to carry their meaning, as in "overstand" and "livity".

J

JahRastafari

The Rastafari name for God, from "Yah", a short form of the Hebrew name of God found in Psalm 68:4 of the King James Bible.

JonkonnuAfrican diaspora

A Jamaican Christmas masquerade with costumed characters, drums, fife and dance, documented from the eighteenth century.

Contested Its origins, between West African masquerade and British mumming, are debated.

K

Kebra NagastEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

The "Glory of Kings", a medieval Ge'ez text telling how the Ethiopian royal line descends from King Solomon and the Queen of Sheba. Rastafari hold it in great esteem.

Contested Historians treat it as a medieval national epic, not a record of events.

KongoAfrican diaspora

Both a people and a historic kingdom of West Central Africa, in parts of today's Angola and the two Congo countries. Kongo ideas underlie Kumina.

KuminaAfrican diaspora

A Kongo-rooted Afro-Jamaican spiritual and musical tradition centred on the ancestors, with drumming, song and dance, strongest in St Thomas.

Kumina

L

LamellophoneAfrican diaspora

An instrument sounded by plucking metal or cane tongues, such as the African mbira and the rumba box used in mento.

Lion of JudahEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

A title from Revelation 5:5. Ethiopian emperors were styled "Conquering Lion of the Tribe of Judah", and the lion is a Rastafari emblem.

LivityRastafari

The Rastafari way of life, covering diet, conduct, speech and care of the body and community.

M

Mansions of RastafariRastafari

The distinct houses or orders within Rastafari, from John 14:2, "In my Father's house are many mansions". They are communities of faith, not music genres, and many Rastafari belong to none.

MaroonAfrican diaspora

A person who escaped slavery, or a descendant of such communities. The word comes from the Spanish cimarrón. Jamaica's Maroons signed treaties with the British in 1739 and 1740.

MentoAfrican diaspora

Jamaica's first commercially recorded popular music, with banjo, guitar, hand drums and the rumba box, and lyrics of comment and humour.

Middle PassageAfrican diaspora

The ocean crossing from Africa to the Americas, the middle leg of the triangular trade in goods and enslaved people.

MyalAfrican diaspora

An Afro-Jamaican set of spiritual and healing traditions, historically linked to some forms of Jamaican ritual practice and often discussed alongside Obeah.

N

Nine NightAfrican diaspora

A Jamaican wake held in the nights after a death, with singing, food and sometimes drumming, to honour the dead and comfort the family.

Nyabinghi (Jamaica)Rastafari

The oldest house of Rastafari, and the name of its great gatherings and three-drum worship. The name is also used for the drumming style itself.

Mansions of RastafariThe Sacred Drum

Nyabingi (East Africa)Rastafari

In the Great Lakes region, a spirit and the movement of mostly female mediums who spoke for it, which became a force of resistance to colonial rule. How its name reached Jamaica is unproven.

Contested No documented chain links the East African tradition to the Jamaican one.

O

ObeahAfrican diaspora

A broad and historically contested Caribbean term for spiritual, healing, protective and other ritual practice, outlawed under colonial rule.

Contested The word's origin, and what it should cover, are debated.

OverstandRastafari

A Rastafari word replacing "understand": to grasp something fully from above, not "stand under" it.

P

Pan-African flagAfrican diaspora

The red, black and green flag adopted by Garvey's UNIA in 1920: red for blood, black for the people, green for the land. It is distinct from the red, gold and green of Ethiopia.

Pan-AfricanismEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

The political idea that people of African descent share a destiny and should act together, for dignity, self-government and unity.

Patwa (Jamaican Creole)African diaspora

The language most Jamaicans speak at home, built mainly on English vocabulary with African influence in its sounds, grammar and some words.

PinnacleRastafari

The first large Rastafari community, founded about 1940 by Leonard Howell in St Catherine, raided in 1941 and in 1954. It received a heritage designation in 2017.

c. 1940 on the timeline

PolitricksRastafari

A Rastafari wordplay on "politics", expressing distrust of political trickery.

Psalm 68:31Ethiopianism and Pan-Africanism

The verse, "Princes shall come out of Egypt; Ethiopia shall soon stretch out her hands unto God", read by Black Christians as a promise for Africa and a cornerstone of Ethiopianism.

R

RastafariRastafari

A faith and way of life that began in 1930s Jamaica, centred on Haile Selassie I as divine and on African dignity and liberation. Many adherents prefer "Rastafari" or "Rasta" and avoid "Rastafarianism".

Contested Rastafari is not one organisation, and members hold varied views.

Ras Tafari MakonnenEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

The birth name and title of the man crowned Haile Selassie I. "Ras", meaning head, was a high noble rank; Tafari was his given name.

ReasoningRastafari

A gathering for close discussion of scripture, history and life, often opened with prayer. Some include the sacramental sharing of the chalice.

Recaptive (liberated African)African diaspora

An African freed from an illegal slave ship by the Royal Navy and landed in a British colony, often then placed in indentured labour. Many reached Jamaica from the 1840s.

Red, gold and greenRastafari

The colours of Ethiopia's flag, adopted by Rastafari. Interpretations vary; red is often said to be the blood of martyrs, gold the wealth of Africa and green its land.

ReparationsAfrican diaspora

The call for redress for slavery and its legacies. Caribbean states through CARICOM have set out an agenda of reparatory justice.

RepatriationEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

The return of the African diaspora to Africa. Rastafari also read it spiritually, and many stress liberation in Jamaica first.

Revival (Pukumina, Pocomania)African diaspora

Jamaican religions that combine Christian worship with African-derived practice, including possession and drumming. They grew from the Great Revival of 1860 to 1861.

Contested The names Pukumina and Pocomania are sometimes considered outsider terms.

S

SankofaAfrican diaspora

An Akan idea, shown in an adinkra symbol, meaning to go back and fetch what you forgot, often used to urge learning from the past.

ShashamaneEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

A town in Ethiopia where land was granted, in a commonly cited 1948 gift, for Black people from the West. It became a home for Rastafari who settled there.

Solomonic dynastyEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

The Ethiopian royal line that traced its descent from Solomon and the Queen of Sheba, restored in 1270 and ending with Haile Selassie I.

SyncretismAfrican diaspora

The blending of religious ideas and practices from different traditions, as when African ritual and Christian worship meet in Revival.

T

TheocracyRastafari

In Nyabinghi usage, the rule of God through Haile Selassie I, as opposed to the rule of Babylon's governments.

Contested Usage and emphasis differ between houses and speakers.

Transatlantic slave tradeAfrican diaspora

The forced shipment of Africans to the Americas between the sixteenth and nineteenth centuries. The Slave Voyages database records roughly 12 million people embarked.

The Atlantic Crossing

Twelve Tribes of IsraelRastafari

A house of Rastafari founded in 1968 by Vernon Carrington (Prophet Gad), which places members in tribes by birth month.

Twelve Tribes archive

U

UNIAEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

The Universal Negro Improvement Association, founded by Marcus Garvey in Jamaica in 1914 and later based in New York, with branches around the world.

W

Windrush generationAfrican diaspora

The Caribbean migrants who came to Britain after the Empire Windrush docked in 1948, among them many Jamaicans. Their sound systems shaped British music.

Z

ZionEthiopianism and Pan-Africanism

Originally the hill of Jerusalem. In Ethiopianism and Rastafari it stands for Africa and Ethiopia as the place of redemption.

ReggaeDNA

The Roots. The Rhythm. The Revolution.

A digital heritage project by African Identity, based in New York City, tracing reggae back through Jamaica to the African peoples, rhythms and ancestral traditions it grew from.

Every page is researched and written in our own words, with each claim labelled as fact, scholarship, belief or oral tradition, and disputed points flagged rather than smoothed over. Sources are listed on each page so claims can be checked.
